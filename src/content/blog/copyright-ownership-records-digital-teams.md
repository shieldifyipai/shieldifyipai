Marketing and product teams publish images, videos, copy, music, software, and design assets at high speed. When an unauthorized copy appears, enforcement can stall because the organization cannot quickly show who created the work, who owns it, or what rights were licensed.

## Register assets at creation or delivery

Assign each important work an internal identifier. Record the title, asset type, creator, creation date, delivery date, source files, publication history, business owner, and storage location.

Keep drafts and project records where they help establish provenance, but apply retention and privacy controls rather than collecting everything indefinitely.

## Connect contracts to the asset

Link employee terms, contractor agreements, assignments, licenses, releases, and agency statements to the works they cover. A master services agreement may not identify a particular photograph or campaign deliverable without a statement of work or acceptance record.

Record effective dates, territory, media, duration, exclusivity, sublicensing, modification, and enforcement authority. Flag unclear terms for legal review.

## Separate owned, licensed, and third-party elements

A single advertisement may combine an owned product image, licensed music, stock footage, a typeface, and user-generated content. Create a rights map showing which elements the organization can enforce and which are controlled by others.

Do not submit claims over an entire composite work when the organization only controls a limited component without reviewing the applicable rights.

## Preserve publication evidence

Record official URLs, publication dates, campaign markets, account identifiers, and archived captures. Publication evidence can help reviewers compare timelines, but being first to publish does not automatically resolve legal ownership.

## Track versions and derivatives

Store relationships between master assets, crops, translations, edits, short-form clips, templates, and regional adaptations. A copied derivative may not match the master file exactly, so the case record should explain the connection.

Use checksums or content fingerprints as integrity and discovery tools. Keep them connected to the underlying source and ownership record.

## Establish an approval process

Before an enforcement request is submitted, require confirmation of:

1. The exact work and copied use.
2. Current owner or exclusive-right holder.
3. Authority of the submitter.
4. Relevant license and exception review.
5. Approved response path.

Assign named reviewers rather than relying on a shared mailbox with unclear responsibility.

## Review the repository after change

Trigger updates after reorganization, acquisition, agency changes, contract renewal, catalog migration, or transfer of rights. Run periodic exception reports for assets missing a creator, agreement, source file, or publication record.

The [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) provides general information on copyrightable works, authorship, ownership, and registration. Legal outcomes depend on the relevant jurisdiction and facts.
