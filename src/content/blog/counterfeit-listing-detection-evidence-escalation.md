A suspicious ecommerce listing can contain useful warning signs, but no single signal proves that a product is counterfeit. Effective review combines product knowledge, seller context, pricing and packaging observations, transaction details, ownership records, and consistent evidence capture.

The goal of a monitoring workflow is to identify listings that deserve attention, prioritize customer risk, and prepare a reliable record for platform, investigative, or legal review.

## Begin with an authentic-product baseline

Reviewers need a reliable reference for genuine products. Maintain an internal product file that includes:

- Approved product names, model numbers, and identifiers.
- Current and historical packaging.
- Authorized product photographs and descriptions.
- Expected labels, seals, serial formats, and security features.
- Normal price ranges and legitimate discount patterns.
- Authorized sellers, distributors, and sales territories.

This baseline helps prevent legitimate older packaging, regional variants, refurbished products, or authorized promotions from being escalated incorrectly.

## Look for clusters of signals

Prioritize listings where several indicators appear together. Common review signals include:

### Product and packaging signals

- Brand names, labels, colors, or packaging that differ from approved references.
- Low-quality printing, inconsistent typography, or visible spelling errors.
- Product photographs that conflict with the title or description.
- Security labels or certificates that appear altered or cannot be verified.
- Identical product images reused by unrelated sellers.

### Price and transaction signals

- Pricing far below typical legitimate ranges without a credible explanation.
- Unusual bulk quantities for scarce, regulated, or premium products.
- Payment instructions that move buyers outside the platform.
- Shipping origins or delivery promises inconsistent with the seller’s claims.

### Seller and account signals

- Recently created accounts offering a large volume of branded products.
- Repeated complaints about authenticity, safety, or non-delivery.
- Seller names, contact details, or imagery linked to previously reviewed accounts.
- Product catalogs combining unrelated brands with duplicated descriptions.
- Frequent account, URL, or identifier changes after earlier reports.

The USPTO’s consumer anti-counterfeiting materials highlight price, packaging, product quality, and place of purchase as useful areas of attention. These are practical indicators, not a substitute for case-specific assessment.

## Preserve the complete listing

A cropped product image rarely tells the full story. Capture the listing in context before contacting the seller or submitting a report.

An evidence record should include:

1. Complete URL and platform name.
2. Listing, product, and seller identifiers.
3. Full-page and detailed screenshots.
4. Listing title, description, price, currency, availability, and quantity.
5. Seller name, profile URL, rating, account age, and contact details where visible.
6. Shipping origin, destination, and delivery claims.
7. Customer reviews or authenticity complaints relevant to the assessment.
8. Capture date, time, time zone, and reviewer.

Preserve the original files and record any automated collection method used. If a listing changes, add a new capture rather than replacing the earlier evidence.

## Connect related sellers and listings

Counterfeit activity may reappear under different accounts. Build connections using observable data such as:

- Reused phone numbers, email addresses, or messaging accounts.
- Shared storefront language and return policies.
- Identical product photographs or watermarks.
- Common shipping locations and business details.
- Repeated pricing patterns and product combinations.
- Redirecting domains, linked social profiles, or advertising accounts.

Treat these connections as investigative leads. Document how each connection was established and avoid presenting an assumption as a verified identity match.

## Separate customer-safety risk from commercial priority

Some findings require faster handling because the suspected product could create health, safety, financial, or identity risks. The [USPTO anti-counterfeiting campaign](https://www.uspto.gov/ip-policy/enforcement-policy/anti-counterfeiting-campaign) notes that fake products can cause real harm, including risks associated with unsafe electronics and contaminated consumer goods.

A triage model can give higher priority to:

- Products used on or inside the body.
- Medicines, medical devices, cosmetics, food, and beverages.
- Batteries, chargers, protective equipment, and safety-critical components.
- Listings impersonating an official store or customer-support channel.
- High-volume or promoted listings with substantial customer reach.

Escalation choices should reflect the relevant platform process, jurisdiction, product risk, and advice from qualified professionals.

## Prepare a platform-ready case package

Before reporting, assemble:

- Relevant trademark or other ownership records.
- Authorization for the submitting representative.
- Genuine-product references.
- Exact listing and seller identifiers.
- Captured evidence and a concise explanation of the observed signals.
- Prior related case references where applicable.

Use factual language. Describe what is visible and why it differs from the authentic baseline. Avoid guaranteeing that the platform will agree or remove the listing; platforms and other third parties make their own decisions under their policies and applicable law.

## Track outcomes and recurrence

After submission, record the platform reference, action date, response, follow-up request, outcome, and closure reason. Link repeat listings to the earlier case so the team can identify recurrence without losing the original timeline.

Useful reporting measures include:

- Findings and confirmed cases by platform.
- High-risk listings by product category.
- Repeat seller and account clusters.
- Average review and response time.
- Accepted, rejected, pending, and appealed reports.
- Reappearance after listing or account action.

## Counterfeit review checklist

- Authentic product and packaging references are current.
- Several independent signals support escalation.
- Listing and seller context is preserved.
- Safety-sensitive findings receive priority.
- Related sellers are connected with documented evidence.
- Ownership and authorization records are ready.
- Platform responses and recurrence are tracked.
