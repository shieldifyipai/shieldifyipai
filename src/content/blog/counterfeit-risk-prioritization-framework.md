Brand teams rarely have equal time for every suspicious listing. A prioritization framework helps reviewers focus on potential customer harm and high-impact activity while preserving lower-confidence findings for appropriate follow-up.

## Separate suspicion from priority

Two concepts are often confused. Confidence describes how strongly the available evidence supports the concern. Priority describes how quickly the case should be reviewed. A low-confidence listing can still be urgent if it involves a safety-critical product or a high-reach advertising campaign.

Record both values and the factors behind them.

## Score customer and product risk

Consider whether the product relates to health, safety, children, regulated uses, electrical performance, protective equipment, or other areas where a defective item could create significant harm. Add the visibility of hazardous claims, missing warnings, or unusual specifications.

This score should trigger specialist review, not a final authenticity determination.

## Measure exposure and transaction capability

Relevant indicators include sales or review counts, search placement, advertising, stock claims, geographic reach, number of variants, marketplace prominence, and whether a customer can complete a purchase.

Visible platform totals may be estimates or manipulated. Preserve them as observations with timestamps.

## Evaluate identity and confusion signals

Score copied packaging, prominent trademark use, official-status claims, store naming, product photography, and language that may suggest authorization. Compare the listing with approved brand and product references.

Allow for legitimate resale, parallel trade, refurbished products, commentary, and other context requiring case-specific review.

## Include evidence readiness

A high-risk case cannot move efficiently if the record lacks a stable URL, seller identifier, ownership documents, or product reference. Use evidence readiness as a workflow factor:

- Ready for authorized review.
- Missing a defined document or capture.
- Requires product authentication.
- Requires local-language or legal review.
- Monitor until more information is available.

## Add recurrence and network context

Prioritize repeat sellers, relisted products, shared identifiers, and coordinated campaigns when the underlying evidence supports the connection. State exactly which observable fields match.

A network label should never substitute for individual listing evidence. Each action should remain traceable to the relevant product page and protected right.

## Use bands, not false precision

A three- or four-level priority model is usually easier to operate than a complex numerical score. Define required response times and owners for each band. Review a sample of decisions monthly to check consistency across analysts.

Override rules should be documented. For example, credible safety reports or active phishing behavior may escalate a case regardless of the total score.

## Report the queue transparently

Show new findings by priority, aged cases, evidence gaps, repeat sellers, and outcomes. Explain changes in methodology before comparing one period with another.

EUIPO research has reported that IP infringement can affect SME turnover, reputation, and competitiveness, while many smaller businesses have limited monitoring capacity. A risk-based queue helps direct limited resources toward the most consequential work.
