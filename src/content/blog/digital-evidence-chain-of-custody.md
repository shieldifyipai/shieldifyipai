Online evidence can change or disappear between detection and review. A chain-of-custody record helps an organization explain what was captured, who handled it, where it was stored, and whether later versions differ from the original.

The appropriate evidentiary standard depends on the forum, jurisdiction, and purpose. An internal operational record is not automatically suitable for litigation, but disciplined handling improves reliability across platform, administrative, and legal review.

## Define the original evidence package

For every capture, record:

- Case and evidence identifiers.
- Source URL or platform identifier.
- Capture date, time, and time zone.
- Capturing person or authorized system.
- Capture method, device, browser, and relevant settings.
- File name, format, size, and cryptographic hash.
- Short description of what the file shows.

Keep the original in a protected location. Work from copies when adding annotations or preparing reports.

## Preserve context and reproducibility

A screenshot without a URL or timestamp may be difficult to interpret. Capture enough of the page to show the platform, account or seller, content, and surrounding context. For dynamic content, record the steps needed to reach the view and relevant territory or login state.

Do not bypass controls or access information without authorization.

## Log every material handling event

Record when evidence is uploaded, downloaded, transferred, exported, annotated, shared, or included in a submission. Each event should identify the actor, time, purpose, and resulting file.

Automated processing such as image comparison, OCR, translation, or format conversion should create a new derivative linked to the original.

## Control access

Use role-based access, strong authentication, and audit logs. Restrict customer, seller, employee, or victim information to people who need it. Separate public-source evidence from confidential ownership records and legal advice where appropriate.

Review access after personnel changes and case closure.

## Manage annotations and redactions

Never overwrite the original. Store a new version with the annotator, date, tool, and purpose. Describe redactions and preserve an access-controlled unredacted copy when policy permits.

Avoid visual changes that could alter meaning, such as removing neighboring text or changing color in a way that obscures the source.

## Verify integrity during transfer

Use hashes to check whether a file changed between capture, storage, and export. Record the transfer channel and recipient. If evidence is printed or incorporated into a PDF, maintain the relationship to the original digital file.

A matching hash supports file-integrity verification; it does not independently prove that the original webpage was authentic or that the underlying claim is correct.

## Apply retention and disposition rules

Define retention based on case status, contractual requirements, applicable law, dispute risk, and privacy obligations. Suspend routine deletion when a legal hold or active dispute requires preservation. At disposition, record what was deleted, when, by whom, and under which policy.
