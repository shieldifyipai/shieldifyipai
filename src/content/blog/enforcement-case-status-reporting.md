Stakeholders need to know what happened after a finding was escalated. A reliable status model should distinguish internal preparation, third-party review, responses, and verified outcomes without implying that the organization controls a platform's final decision.

## Define a controlled status model

Use statuses with clear entry and exit rules. A practical sequence may include:

- New finding.
- Under internal review.
- Evidence incomplete.
- Ready for authorized action.
- Submitted.
- Third-party review pending.
- More information requested.
- Accepted, rejected, or partially actioned.
- Closed with a documented reason.
- Monitoring for recurrence.

Avoid a generic “resolved” status that hides whether content was removed, changed, rejected, or simply no longer visible.

## Separate status from outcome

Status explains where the workflow is now. Outcome explains what was observed at the end of a defined review period. Keep platform response, current content state, and internal closure reason in separate fields.

A listing that disappears before a platform responds should not automatically be recorded as a successful takedown. Use a state such as “no longer observed; cause unconfirmed.”

## Record ownership and next action

Every open case should have a current owner, next action, and due date. Record dependencies such as missing authorization, product authentication, local counsel, platform clarification, or customer information.

Queue reports should identify cases without an owner or next action.

## Standardize response reasons

Map third-party messages into a controlled response taxonomy while preserving the original text. Categories may include accepted, insufficient rights information, incomplete URLs, authorization required, exception or dispute raised, duplicate, out of scope, or no response.

This makes trends visible without losing the provider's exact wording.

## Build aging views

Report age from detection, first review, submission, and last response. Different clocks answer different questions. A case detected early but held for ownership documentation should not be analyzed as a slow platform response.

Use median and percentile views rather than only averages, and separate priority levels.

## Link every metric to evidence

Dashboards should allow reviewers to open the underlying case, source capture, submission, and response. Define each metric, denominator, time period, and excluded status.

Do not publish removal rates that silently exclude pending or rejected cases. Clear definitions protect credibility.

## Report learning, not just volume

Add recurring rejection causes, repeated sellers or accounts, evidence-readiness gaps, channels with growing queues, and monitoring rules that produced useful findings. Leadership can then decide where to improve process, documentation, or coverage.
