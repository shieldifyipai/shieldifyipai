Search advertisements are dynamic. Results can vary by query, location, device, language, time, account state, and auction conditions. A reliable monitoring process preserves those variables and connects the advertisement to its destination experience.

## Define the monitored query set

Group queries by purpose:

- Exact brand and product names.
- Brand plus “official,” “support,” “login,” or “download.”
- Brand plus commercial terms such as “buy,” “discount,” or “outlet.”
- Common misspellings and transliterations.
- Campaign phrases and product model combinations.

Record the territory, language, device profile, and schedule for each group. Monitoring frequency should reflect customer exposure and observed risk.

## Capture the advertisement in context

A useful record includes the complete results page, search query, timestamp, location context, ad position, advertiser name or verification information where shown, headline, description, visible URL, extensions, and destination URL.

Preserve a focused image of the advertisement and a wider capture showing surrounding results. Do not crop away labels that indicate whether the placement is sponsored.

## Follow the destination carefully

Record redirects and the final landing page. Compare the visible domain with the destination domain, certificate information, page branding, products, forms, contact details, and payment options.

Some advertisements lead to legitimate resellers, review sites, or comparative content. Others may use a familiar brand term while clearly identifying an independent source. Document what the user actually sees rather than inferring intent from the query alone.

## Connect trademark and customer-risk review

Trademark analysis may consider the mark, goods or services, presentation, territory, and likelihood of confusion. Customer-risk analysis may focus on credential collection, deceptive support claims, payment requests, or unsafe products. Keep the two analyses distinct and route urgent security issues in parallel.

The [USPTO trademark overview](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark) explains that trademark rights relate to how a sign identifies the source of particular goods or services, not ownership of a word in every context.

## Select the correct reporting route

Possible routes include the advertising platform's trademark or policy process, a legal content-removal request, reports to the destination host or registrar, and internal security escalation. Google notes that legal removal requests should identify the correct product, legal basis, and specific URLs rather than only a site homepage.

Maintain separate records for the advertisement and landing page because each may have a different identifier, operator, and response channel.

## Measure recurrence

Track repeated advertiser names, destination domains, redirect chains, creative language, and query coverage. Record whether an advertisement disappeared because of action, auction variation, budget timing, or an unknown reason. An absent ad during a later search does not prove permanent removal.
