Social media impersonation review should answer two separate questions: what the account is doing, and what evidence supports an appropriate response. A similar name or logo may justify review, but context determines urgency and reporting path.

## Record the account identity

Capture the platform, complete profile URL, unique account identifier where visible, handle, display name, creation indicators, profile image, biography, location, follower counts, verification status, and visible contact details.

Take screenshots of the full profile and individual high-risk posts. Record the time zone used for captures because posts, stories, and advertisements can disappear quickly.

## Compare the account with official channels

Use a current inventory of approved profiles, regional accounts, agencies, employees, partners, and campaign handles. Check whether the account is clearly labeled as commentary, fan activity, parody, or an independent reseller.

Do not rely on verification badges alone. Platform verification systems and badge meanings can change, and unverified official accounts may exist.

## Review behavior and customer journey

Look beyond visual similarity. Relevant behaviors may include:

- Claims of official customer support or employment.
- Requests to move conversations to private messages.
- Links to lookalike domains, forms, downloads, or payment pages.
- Giveaways, investment offers, discount codes, or recruitment claims.
- Use of copied posts to create a history of apparent legitimacy.
- Responses to customers who intended to contact the real brand.

Follow links in a controlled environment and avoid providing personal information.

## Assess reach and urgency

Record post engagement, advertising indicators, follower growth, customer comments, and whether the account is actively contacting users. A small account with a credential-harvesting link may deserve higher priority than a large inactive fan page.

Use consistent severity criteria tied to customer harm, transaction capability, identity confusion, reach, and evidence quality.

## Prepare a platform-ready record

Connect the account to the trademark, ownership record, official profile, and authorized representative. Identify the precise profile or post URLs being reported and explain the misleading behavior in factual terms.

Platforms may provide different paths for impersonation, trademark, fraud, privacy, or copyright. Choose the path that matches the observed issue instead of submitting the same generic text everywhere.

## Track linked infrastructure and recurrence

Record destination domains, phone numbers, messaging handles, email addresses, creative assets, and other public signals. Compare them with earlier reviewed cases, but describe relationships as observed overlaps unless stronger evidence supports a conclusion.

After action, record the platform response, status changes, account renaming, removal, and any replacement profiles. Feed new naming patterns back into monitoring rules.
