## Scenario

A financial-services brand finds lookalike social profiles, sponsored posts, and domains that use similar names, logos, or visual styling. Some may be commentary or unrelated uses; others may direct visitors toward forms or contact channels that deserve urgent review.

This illustrative workflow explains how findings can be organized. It does not identify a real organization, confirm fraud, or promise a platform or registrar outcome.

## 1. Document official brand signals

Reviewers begin with official accounts, domains, approved campaign pages, trademarks, visual references, and authorized partners. This reference set helps prevent legitimate activity from entering the enforcement queue.

## 2. Capture the full user journey

The record includes the profile or domain, visible brand use, advertisements, destination links, contact methods, forms, redirects, and screenshots. Reviewers avoid submitting sensitive personal information through suspicious pages.

## 3. Apply customer-risk criteria

Priority increases when a finding appears to solicit credentials or payment, imitates official support, uses paid distribution, reaches a large audience, or connects to a cluster of related assets. These are triage signals, not final legal conclusions.

## 4. Route the case appropriately

Depending on the facts, the case may require platform reporting, registrar or host review, advertising-channel escalation, internal security coordination, or qualified legal assessment.

## 5. Monitor recurrence

Names, imagery, destination infrastructure, contact details, and response history are recorded so new findings can be compared with earlier cases without assuming a connection that the evidence does not support.
