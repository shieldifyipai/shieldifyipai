## Scenario

A media team finds product photography, written material, and short-form video reused across websites and social accounts. The same asset may appear in different crops, edits, and combinations, making it difficult to connect every finding to the correct source record.

This illustrative example demonstrates an evidence workflow. It is not legal advice, a client testimonial, or a claim that a particular reuse is infringing.

## 1. Map the original works

The team creates a rights inventory containing the original files, creation or first-publication context, ownership records, licenses, and authorized uses. Each asset receives a stable internal identifier.

## 2. Preserve the external use

Reviewers record the public URL, account or domain, timestamp, surrounding context, screenshots, and available metadata. Video and multi-page content require enough context to show where and how the material appears.

## 3. Build factual comparisons

The case record places the original and external use side by side. Notes describe visible similarities, edits, attribution, commercial context, and known permissions without making unsupported legal conclusions.

## 4. Confirm authority and exceptions

Before escalation, the team confirms who owns the work, who may act, whether a license exists, and whether qualified counsel should review possible exceptions or jurisdiction-specific requirements.

## 5. Maintain the timeline

Submission details, platform responses, counter-notices, follow-up requests, reappearances, and closure reasons remain attached to the same case so the operating history is not lost.
