## Scenario

A consumer brand discovers many marketplace listings using its registered mark. Some appear to be authorized resale, some contain incomplete product information, and others show patterns that deserve closer review. The volume makes one-off reporting difficult to manage and easy to duplicate.

This illustrative case shows how a team could turn those findings into a structured review queue. It does not describe a named client engagement or claim that any listing is unlawful.

## 1. Establish the review baseline

The workflow begins with the current trademark records, authentic product references, authorized seller information, territories, and channel-specific priorities. Reviewers need this baseline before assigning risk or choosing an action.

## 2. Capture each listing consistently

Each finding receives a source URL, timestamp, seller identifier, product title, visible mark use, price, images, shipping location, and screenshots. The record distinguishes observed facts from reviewer notes.

## 3. Connect related seller signals

Repeated contact details, product photography, naming patterns, fulfillment locations, and storefront links can be recorded as relationships. These signals support prioritization but are not treated as proof on their own.

## 4. Prioritize professional review

Listings can be grouped by customer risk, recurrence, evidence completeness, and apparent relationship to other findings. Higher-priority records move to ownership and authorization review before any platform submission.

## 5. Track actions and outcomes

Every submission, platform reference, response, follow-up request, and closure reason remains connected to the original evidence. This creates an auditable case history and reduces duplicate work.
